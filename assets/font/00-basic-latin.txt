# Basic Latin — the printable ASCII, and the shapes every other block is measured against.
#
# Capitals are five wide and seven tall, which is the smallest cell in which the Latin alphabet is
# unambiguous. Four wide loses B against 8 and G against 6; six wide buys nothing a reader can use
# and costs a fifth more voxels on every sign in the world.
#
# Lowercase has an x-height of five and hangs two below the baseline where it needs to, so `p` and
# `o` sit on the same line and `b` and `o` share a top. `top` is how many rows above the baseline
# the glyph's FIRST row sits at: 7 for a capital or an ascender, 5 for a plain lowercase letter.
#
# `advance` is how far the pen moves, INCLUDING the glyph's own width. It is only written where a
# letter is narrower than five — `i` and `l` are two and three wide, and setting them five wide
# would put a hole either side of every one.
#
# A row is drawn with `#` and `.`. Anything that is not a directive inside an open glyph is a row,
# so a row of dots is a row of dots and can never be read as a comment.
