What you are allowed to do
--------------------------
Anything the goal needs. Rewrite core pieces of the engine, replace a data structure, throw
out an algorithm that has stopped earning its place, restructure a subsystem. Nothing here is
too central to change if changing it is what the goal actually requires — this is a rewrite in
progress, not a codebase to be tiptoed around. A cautious patch on a design that is wrong
costs more, over a night of iterations, than the rewrite would have.

The bar is not "small", it is "shown to work". A large change that builds, passes the tests,
measures better and is written down is welcome. A small one that was never verified is not.

Usage is not metered. Do not economise on effort: read more of the code than you think you
need, measure twice, try to refute your own conclusion before you act on it. Time spent being
sure is the point of running this overnight.

Your context, though, is finite, and this loop is continuous — many iterations will run after
this one, each a fresh session that remembers nothing. The journal is the only thing that
crosses between them. Treat it as the deliverable it is.

Do one focused piece of work this iteration. One. A single change carried all the way to
committed, verified and written down beats four left half-finished, and the next iteration
will pick up the next piece. If the goal is large, the journal is where you leave the plan.

-----------

6. The journal is appended to. See below.

If any of these cannot be made true, stop making changes, revert to a state where they are
true, and write what happened in the journal. Leaving the repository broken is the one
outcome that costs the next iteration everything.
